# AI Usage Note — FulfillFlow

I used AI as a development assistant, mainly for brainstorming the information architecture, generating a first-pass implementation structure, and checking that the application covered the main operational problems in the project brief.

I did not want the application to become a complicated warehouse-management system. I kept the first version focused on the problems that affect daily execution most: knowing what needs attention, seeing stock risk before picking, tracking exceptions, and making courier handoffs visible.

### Where I changed the AI suggestion

**1. Scope:** A broader first idea could have included many automation features and integrations. I reduced the scope to a small operational dashboard because the brief says there are no real store or courier systems available. A simple working demo is more useful for the reviewer than simulated integrations.

**2. Warehouse usability:** I avoided a dense analytics-heavy interface. The brief says the warehouse team is experienced but not very comfortable with technology, so I used short labels, clear status badges, large action buttons and a small number of screens.

### My judgement

The sample data is intentionally operational rather than purely decorative. It includes a priority order, a missed promise, a stock mismatch between the main and overflow warehouse, staged boxes and courier cut-offs. These examples allow the reviewer to see how the application would help the team make decisions during a normal working day.
